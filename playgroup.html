Our Programmes/Playgroup
Playgroup · 2–3 years · 2 hours 30 minutes

The first time they’re brave without you.

Playgroup is less about what your child learns and more about what they discover they can do — including the very large thing of being somewhere lovely without you in the room.

Book a visitSee a session
2–3years old 2½hours, and no rush

Let’s talk about the first week.

Some children walk in, spot the water tray and forget to say goodbye. Others cry at the door for four days running.

Both are completely normal, and neither predicts anything about how the year will go.

What we’ve learned across 45 centres is that separation goes best when nobody pretends it isn’t happening. So we don’t distract your child while you slip away — that tends to make the next morning worse. We say goodbye properly, we stay with them through it, and we message you once they’ve settled. Which, in the overwhelming majority of cases, is within about ten minutes.

You can call whenever you like. Genuinely. Most parents call on day two and then stop.

2½hrs

Two and a half hours, and roughly this shape.

Playgroup · 2 hrs 30 mins

  1. 01

    Arrival and settling

    Coats, shoes, a familiar face at the door, and no rush.

  2. 02

    Circle time

    Names, a song, the weather, whose turn it is — the beginning of belonging to a group.

  3. 03

    Free exploration

    Sand, water, dough, blocks, dressing up. Adults nearby, not hovering.

  4. 04

    Snack

    Washing hands, sitting together, opening one’s own box. A surprising amount of learning.

  5. 05

    Outdoor or movement

    Climbing, running, balancing — because a two-year-old’s brain lives partly in their legs.

  6. 06

    Story and wind-down

    The same books over and over, on purpose.

The order flexes. If a group is deep in something at snack time, snack waits.

It looks like mess. Here’s the plan underneath.

Every activity in the session above is doing at least one of these jobs. Usually several.

How DISCOVER works
Language
From single words to short sentences; naming things rather than pointing at them.
Independence
Shoes, hand-washing, snack boxes, putting things back.
Gross motor
Climbing, balancing, carrying something heavy across a room.
Fine motor
Pincer grip through dough, threading, pouring, picking up small things.
Social
Playing beside other children first, then with them. That shift is the year’s big event.
Emotional
The beginnings of naming a feeling instead of being flattened by it.
By the end of the year

Things you’ll probably notice at home.

  • They separate at the door without it being an ordeal.
  • They ask for what they want in words, at least some of the time.
  • They can be part of a group without needing an adult all to themselves.
  • They take their own shoes off, and are extremely pleased about it.
  • They have a favourite person at school who isn’t a teacher.

Starting out

My child has never been away from me. Will she cope?
Almost certainly, and probably faster than you will. Some children take a fortnight. We’ll agree a settling plan with you, we won’t distract her while you slip away, and we’ll message you once she’s settled — which is usually within ten minutes of the door closing.
What age should we start?
There’s no single right answer. Two to three is typical for Playgroup, but a child who’s had a lot of group experience may be ready earlier, and one who hasn’t may do better with another few months. Tell us about your child and we’ll give you an honest view.

The day

What are the timings?
Playgroup and Nursery run 2 hours 30 minutes. Kidzo Junior and Kidzo Senior run 3 hours 30 minutes. Exact start times vary by centre. Daycare runs 8:00am to 8:00pm.
Do you provide transport?
No. We’ve chosen not to run transport, and our centres are located to be close to the neighbourhoods they serve.

Learning

Will my child learn to read and write?
Yes. Letter sounds and mark-making begin in Kidzo Junior, and by the end of Kidzo Senior most children read simple words and write their name and beyond. What we don’t do is start formal writing at three, because pushing it early reliably costs more than it gains.
Isn’t it all just play?
It’s all play, and none of it is just play. Every activity in the room is planned against a developmental objective.

How will I know if she’s progressing?
Through structured observation against developmental milestones, documented work, and parent-teacher meetings that walk you through all of it. If something concerns us you’ll hear it early and in person, never in an end-of-term report.
How much screen time is there?
Tablets and projectors are used for short, planned, specific purposes and never to occupy a room.

Come and see it. Honestly, it’s the only way to know.

Book a visit at your nearest centre. Walk the rooms while a session is actually happening, not after everyone’s gone home. Meet the person who’d be spending the day with your child. And ask the difficult questions — how many adults per room, what happens when somebody gets hurt, what you do with a child who won’t settle.

Those are the ones we most like answering.

Our Programmes/Kidzo Junior
Kidzo Junior · 4–5 years · 3 hours 30 minutes

Where play meets its first real concepts.

This is the year letters and numbers turn up — and the year it matters enormously how they turn up.

Book a visitSee a session
4–5years old 3½hours, building stamina

“But when do they start learning properly?”

Now. And this is the year where the method makes the biggest difference.

A four-year-old can be drilled into recognising letters. It works, in the narrow sense that they’ll recognise letters. What it costs is harder to see at the time: children taught this way frequently arrive at six able to decode words without much interest in reading them, and with an early, quiet conviction that this subject is a chore.

So at Kidzonia, letters arrive through sounds, songs, stories and games. Numbers arrive through counting things that are actually there and worth counting. The concepts are identical to the ones on any worksheet. The difference is that your child is still curious at the end of it.

The day also gets longer here — three and a half hours — because the stamina for a full school day is itself something that has to be built.

What they’re learning.

Every activity in the session above is doing at least one of these jobs. Usually several.

How DISCOVER works
Phonics
Letter sounds, blending, initial and final sounds.
Early numeracy
Counting with understanding, number recognition, more and less, simple addition through objects.
Pre-writing
Grip, control, patterns, then letters.
Comprehension
Answering questions about a story, predicting what happens next.
Executive function
Planning, sequencing, and returning to something started yesterday.
Social
Cooperation, rules, resolving a disagreement without an adult.
By the end of the year

Things you’ll probably notice at home.

  • They recognise letters and the sounds they make.
  • They count with real understanding, not just recitation.
  • They write their name, and attempt other words with confidence.
  • They finish something they began the day before.
  • They explain their reasoning — “it sank because it’s heavy.”

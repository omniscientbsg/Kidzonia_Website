Admissions · For parents

Joining is straightforward. Deciding is the hard part.

So we’ve made the process simple and put everything you need to know on one page — including the questions you should be asking us.

From first enquiry to first morning.

  1. 01Get in touch

    Fill in the enquiry form or call the centre directly. We’ll come back to you within one working day.

  2. 02Visit the centre

    Come and meet the centre head. Walk the rooms while a session is running. Ask everything. This usually takes about forty minutes and there’s no obligation attached to it.

  3. 03Confirm the place

    If it feels right, we’ll confirm availability in the programme that suits your child. If we think a different programme would suit them better, we’ll say so.

  4. 04Complete the form

    The admission form, plus the documents listed below.

  5. 05Submit and pay

    Return the form with the confirmation fee, and you’re enrolled. We’ll then talk to you about settling in — which is the part that actually matters.

Age criteria.

Age is a guide, not a rule. A child who has never been in a group before may settle far better starting a programme below their age, and we’d rather tell you that than fill a place.

What to bring

Documents you’ll need.

Bring these with the admission form at step four.

  • Completed application form
  • Birth certificate or passport — certified copy, for date of birth
  • Five passport-size photographs of your child
  • Three stamp-size photographs of your child
  • Three passport-size photographs of each parent
  • ID and address proof for both parents

Settling in

The first two weeks.

This is the part most admissions pages skip, and it’s the part parents worry about most.

Every child settles differently and there’s very little correlation between a difficult first week and how the year goes. We’ll agree a settling plan with you — sometimes shorter sessions to begin with, sometimes a familiar object from home, sometimes just consistency and patience.

What we won’t do is distract your child while you leave. It seems kinder in the moment and it reliably makes the following morning harder.

You can call whenever you want to. Most parents call on day two, and then stop.

Questions worth asking any preschool. Including us.

You’re going to visit several places and they’ll all seem lovely. These are the questions that actually separate them.

  1. 1

    How many adults are in the room right now?

  2. 2

    Can I see a session running, rather than an empty classroom?

  3. 3

    What happens when a child won’t settle?

  4. 4

    What did you do the last time a child was hurt here?

  5. 5

    How long has the teacher in this room worked for you?

  6. 6

    Who is allowed to collect my child, and how do you verify it?

  7. 7

    What do you do when a child is behind where you’d expect?

  8. A good centre head answers all seven without pausing. If you get vague answers anywhere — including here — that’s your answer.

Book a visit

Come and see an ordinary morning.

Not a tidied-up one. Ask to visit mid-session, while it’s noisy — that’s when you learn something about a place.

  • About forty minutes, and there’s no obligation at the end of it
  • You’ll meet the centre head, and ideally the teacher who’d have your child
  • You’ll see rooms in use, not empty ones
  • Bring your child if you can — how they respond to a room tells you a lot
  • Ask everything, including at other preschools
